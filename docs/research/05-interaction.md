# 05. Interaction design (HCI)

How people use gaze to point and click, and how to measure whether it helps. Read before M3 step 7 (Fitts task) and before any M4 work on clicking or snapping.

## MAGIC pointing (gaze moves the cursor, the hand finishes)
**Touches:** the Cursor-mode design (Right Command tap warps to gaze, trackpad finishes); M3 step 7; the "productivity" direction.
**The science:** Zhai, Morimoto and Ihde (CHI 1999) proposed letting gaze carry the cursor across the screen and the hand do the fine positioning, because gaze is fast but imprecise and the hand is precise but slow over distance. Two variants: **liberal** (the cursor warps to every new fixation more than 120 px from it) and **conservative** (the cursor appears near the gaze only when the hand starts moving). In their pilot (9 people) the throughput was manual 3.2, conservative 4.55 and liberal 4.76 bits/s; mean times were 1.40 s manual, 1.52 s conservative, 1.33 s liberal. People liked the liberal one more but found warps during reading distracting. The tracker in that study was far from perfect; the method was built for an imprecise tracker.
**What it means for us:** our design is a known, tested technique: a conservative-style MAGIC with an explicit trigger (a key instead of the start of a hand movement). The literature predicts that the gain depends on **distance**: for a near target, the trackpad alone is as fast, and the correction after a 2-5 deg warp error eats the saving. The gain should appear for far targets, big screens and second monitors, which is the "power user" case. It also says a 2-5 deg tracker is good enough for this use, which is not true for clicking by gaze alone.
**What to try:** R18 (in step 7's Fitts task: trackpad only vs gaze-jump + trackpad, across target distances and widths, with throughput per condition and time split into the warp and the correction phase; pre-register the expected break-even distance from the current error; also log how far the trackpad correction moves after each warp, which is a free per-warp error measure).
**Confidence and sources:** high that the technique works in principle; the numbers come from a 9-person pilot with a 1999 tracker. [Zhai, Morimoto and Ihde 1999](https://latin.ime.usp.br/media/papers/pdf/MAGIC.pdf).

## The Midas touch and dwell
**Touches:** clicking without hands (accessibility direction, M4); why Cursor mode never clicks by gaze.
**The science:** Jacob (CHI 1990) named the core problem: the eyes look at things all the time, so if looking means acting, everything you look at gets activated ("Midas touch"). The fixes are a separate trigger (a key, a blink, a switch, a sound) or **dwell**: act only after gaze stays on a target for a set time. Short dwells (Jacob used about 150-250 ms) work only when mistakes are cheap and easy to undo; consequential actions need longer dwells or a confirmation step. In gaze typing, dwell times are usually adjustable and become shorter as users gain practice.
**What it means for us:** our live target test is already a dwell test: holding the dot in a box for 0.5 s hit 67-100% of 6 deg boxes and 50-100% of 4 deg boxes, with first entry after 0.6-0.9 s. So a hands-free click is realistic only on targets of roughly 5-6 deg or larger (about 5-6 cm at 60 cm), which most macOS buttons are not. That is why hands-free clicking needs snapping (next section) and an undo, and why the productivity path should keep the hand for the click.
**What to try:** R26 (M4, only if the accessibility direction is chosen: a dwell-click prototype with adjustable dwell, a visible dwell progress ring, and an "undo last click" key; measured on the step-7 task with target sizes from 2 to 8 deg).
**Confidence and sources:** high. [Jacob 1990, "What you look at is what you get"](https://doi.org/10.1145/97243.97246); a recent analysis of Midas-touch failures for gaze vs hand: [arXiv 2603.15991](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.15991).

## Fitts' law and ISO 9241-411 throughput
**Touches:** M3 step 7 (usability task); how to compare gaze-jump with the trackpad fairly.
**The science:** Fitts' law says the time to point at a target grows with the index of difficulty `ID = log2(D / W + 1)` (distance D, width W). The standard (ISO 9241-411, following MacKenzie) uses the **effective** values actually achieved: `We = 4.133 x SD` of the landing points along the movement axis, `De` = mean movement distance, `IDe = log2(De / We + 1)`, and **throughput** `TP = IDe / MT` in bits/s, averaged per participant over conditions. Throughput combines speed and accuracy, so a technique cannot look good by being fast and sloppy. Typical mouse throughput is about 4-5 bits/s; trackpads are lower.
**What it means for us:** step 7 should report throughput, not only time, and compute it per condition with the effective width. Because a gaze warp is not a Fitts-like movement (its time hardly depends on distance), the model will fit the trackpad condition well and the gaze condition less well; that misfit is itself the finding, and splitting the time into warp and correction phases makes it visible. Our lag analysis (01) says to decompose latency (camera, pipeline, filter, remainder) instead of subtracting an assumed eye reaction time (R17).
**What to try:** R18 (above) and R17 (01).
**Confidence and sources:** high. [MacKenzie, Fitts' law notes](https://www.yorku.ca/mack/UCLAN2018/FittsLaw.pdf); [MacKenzie, FittsStudy and ISO 9241-411 practice](https://www.yorku.ca/mack/hcii2015a.html).

## Target expansion (bubble cursor) and snap-to-target
**Touches:** snap-to-target (M4); making a 2-5 deg tracker click small buttons.
**The science:** the bubble cursor (Grossman and Balakrishnan, CHI 2005) grows its activation area so it always contains exactly one target: each target's clickable area becomes its **Voronoi region**, the area closer to it than to any other target, without changing what the screen shows. It was about 30% faster than a point cursor in 1D and about 14% faster in 2D (0.93 vs 1.08 s), and 34% faster than object pointing. The gain shrinks as targets get denser, because the regions get smaller.
**What it means for us:** this is the standard way to turn a coarse pointer into a reliable one: if two clickable things are further apart than our error, snapping to the nearest one removes the error entirely. macOS knows where clickable elements are through the Accessibility API, so the targets do not have to be guessed from the image. Where targets are dense (toolbars, text), snapping helps less and the hand or a zoom step must finish.
**What to try:** R19 (M4: snap-to-nearest-clickable via the macOS Accessibility API, measured on the step-7 task and on real Cursor-mode clicks as "would the snapped target have been the clicked one", computed offline from logs before any live version; report how often the nearest target is ambiguous given the current error).
**Confidence and sources:** high for the technique; its fit with gaze error and real macOS layouts is untested. [Grossman and Balakrishnan 2005](https://www.dgp.toronto.edu/papers/tgrossman_CHI2005.pdf).

## Implicit correction from normal use (EyeO)
**Touches:** drift within a session; click learning; recalibration burden; L4.
**The science:** EyeO (Microsoft Research, 2023) observed that gaze-typing users compensate for a miscalibrated tracker by looking slightly off target when *selecting*, but not when *reading* what they typed. So whenever the user fixates (over 100 ms) within 150 px of the last typed character, the offset from that character is a calibration label. A moving average over 64 such samples, clipped to +-200 px, corrects the cursor. Results with 19 people: faster typing (22.7 vs 20.3 characters/min), far fewer aborted attempts (0.05 vs 0.6), lower frustration and effort, and 14 of 19 preferred it. Users asked for an on/off switch and to be told when calibration changes.
**What it means for us:** this is the same idea as our click learning, with a different implicit label. It also points to labels we are not using: the **trackpad correction after a warp** (from where the cursor landed to where the user clicked) and **reading the text just typed** (the caret position is available from the Accessibility API). Their design choices (a distance gate, a fixation requirement, a long moving average, a clipped correction) are close to ours, which is reassuring, and their users' requests (on/off, transparency) apply directly.
**What to try:** R25 (exploratory first: from existing Cursor logs, measure warp landing vs click position as a second label stream next to clicks; later, a caret-based reading label while typing; any live use goes through a pre-registered comparison like round 4).
**Confidence and sources:** high for the study (19 people, a commercial infrared tracker, gaze typing); transfer to a webcam and to pointing is untested. [Saran et al. 2023, EyeO](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2307.15039).
